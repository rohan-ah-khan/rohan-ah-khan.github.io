Machine learning models were trained on 16S rRNA gene data from marine sediments in the Gulf of Mexico and Scotian Slope to predict hydrocarbon seepage. Gradient Boosting Machines performed best, identifying Aminicenantia and Sulfurovum as key indicators in the Gulf of Mexico, and Campylobacterota and JS1 (Caldatribacteriota) in the Scotian Slope. While cross-basin predictions were inconsistent, combining data and filtering for important features improved accuracy, showing the potential of microbial community profiles for detecting hydrocarbon seeps.
