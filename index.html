About

{% capture about_md %} I am a bioinformatician focused on supporting diverse research programs and building reusable tools for omics data analysis. My work spans a range of genomic applications, including microbiome research, rare disease genomics, and cancer genomics. I have experience developing end-to-end workflows for variant calling, annotation, and large-scale data analysis, as well as applying machine learning approaches to biological datasets. Currently, I’m an intern at SickKids’ Centre for Computational Medicine, a core bioinformatics facility within the hospital. There, I contribute to the development and maintenance of genome analysis pipelines used in clinical research workflows. My work includes evaluating ensemble approaches for long-read structural variant calling, VCF annotation and harmonization, and building reproducible Snakemake pipelines designed for HPC environments. I also develop containerized tools and database-backed systems for storing and querying genomic and literature data, including natural language–based retrieval. Previously, at Princess Margaret Cancer Centre, I worked on follicular lymphoma research, developing an end-to-end tumor-only sequencing pipeline integrating multi-caller SNV consensus strategies and CNV detection. This pipeline supported large-scale cohort analyses linking genomic alterations to treatment response and survival outcomes. I began my research career at the University of Calgary in microbial genomics, where I combined 16S rRNA sequencing and machine learning to predict hydrocarbon presence in marine and industrial systems. That experience motivated my interest in building computational systems that make complex genomic data more interpretable and actionable.
