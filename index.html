About

{% capture about_md %} I’m a bioinformatics researcher interested in translating complex genomic data into systems that are usable, scalable, and impactful. My work sits at the intersection of genomics, computation, and infrastructure, building tools and pipelines that turn raw sequencing data into interpretable insights for exploratory research. Currently, I’m an intern at SickKids’ Centre for Computational Medicine, a core bioinformatics facility within the hospital. There, I help develop and maintain genome analysis pipelines used in clinical research workflows. My work includes long-read structural variant calling and benchmarking, VCF annotation and harmonization, and building reproducible Snakemake pipelines designed for HPC environments. Previously, at Princess Margaret Cancer Centre, I worked on follicular lymphoma research, developing an end-to-end tumor-only sequencing pipeline integrating multi-caller SNV consensus strategies and CNV detection. The pipeline supported downstream survival and treatment response analyses across international cohorts. I began my research career at the University of Calgary in microbial genomics, where I combined 16S rRNA sequencing and machine learning to predict hydrocarbon presence in marine and industrial systems.
